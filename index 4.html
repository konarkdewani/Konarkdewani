About Me

“I want to understand the world before I try to change it.”

I am Konark Dewani, an Economics student at the University of Delhi. My academic journey has taken me through economics, econometrics, international politics and the questions that shape societies and institutions.

Beyond the classroom, I enjoy following geopolitics and global affairs, creating digital content, reading, sports and the quieter creative side of life. I believe curiosity is one of the most valuable habits a person can cultivate.

A major part of my creative work is Global Affairs India, an Instagram channel where I explore geopolitics, international affairs and current events. The aim is simple: make important global stories easier to understand while encouraging people to look beyond headlines.

Why We Need to Read Beyond the Headline

We live in a time when information is everywhere. Every day, we scroll through hundreds of posts, news updates, videos and headlines. Most of the time, we see a headline, form an opinion within seconds, and move on. But a headline rarely tells the whole story.

Headlines are designed to grab our attention. Sometimes they give us the most important part of a story, but sometimes they leave out important details or present an issue in a way that creates a particular reaction. If we judge an entire situation only by its headline, we may end up believing something that is incomplete or even misleading.

Reading beyond the headline means taking a few extra minutes to understand what actually happened. It means looking at the full article, checking the facts, understanding the background and, when necessary, comparing different sources. This is especially important when the topic is politics, international affairs, conflict, economics or any issue where a single sentence cannot explain the entire situation.

It also helps us avoid reacting emotionally. A shocking headline can make us angry, worried or excited before we even know the facts. By reading the complete story, we give ourselves a chance to understand the situation instead of simply reacting to it.

This does not mean that every article is completely unbiased or that every source is reliable. It means that we should be willing to question what we see and look for more information before forming a strong opinion.

In the age of social media, attention has become more valuable than ever. But being informed is not about knowing the most headlines. It is about understanding what lies behind them.

So, the next time a headline catches your attention, don't stop there.

Read the story. Understand the context. Then form your opinion.
